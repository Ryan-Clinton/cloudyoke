# 10 to 11 November 2026: high cloud the airports couldn't see

The first night the reference sources were compared with a telescope's own view of the sky. There is
no cloudyoke station yet, so the "sensor" here is a telescope's guide camera near Ballymoney: how many
stars it could find. All times UTC.

## What each source said

**The telescope:** a frame refused at 19:09 for stars 47% dimmer, thin cloud at 20:30 with a few
bright stars visible by eye, and from 22:35 to the end of the night at 02:50 the guide camera found
only 0 to 6 stars, against dozens in a clear sky.

**The airports** (from `data/reference/`):

- City of Derry (EGAE, 42 km west, observed by a person): CAVOK at 18:20 and 18:50, then a few to
  broken at 2,200 to 2,800 ft until it closed at 22:00. European reports leave out cloud above
  5,000 ft, so these say nothing about high cloud. Closed overnight.
- Belfast International (EGAA, 50 km south-south-east, automatic): NCD, no cloud detected, every
  half hour from 19:20 to 05:20. Its ceilometer reaches about 12,000 ft.
- Belfast City (EGAC, automatic): scattered at 2,800 ft at 20:20, a little at 1,900 to 2,100 ft
  after 04:20, otherwise NCD. Prestwick: nothing.

So no airport reported any cloud above 2,800 ft all night.

**The satellite** (telescopeyoke's `clouds.py`): cloud over the site in every check from 22:10 to
02:10.

**Met Éireann's ceilometers**, which measure up to about 25,000 ft:

- Malin Head (about 50 km north-west): a layer at about 22,000 ft for a few minutes at 18:30, then
  at about 24,000 ft from 21:00 to 22:30, alongside low cloud at 2,000 ft. From 23:00 to 05:30, no
  layer at all.
- Finner (Donegal Bay, south-west): layers at 17,000 to 25,000 ft every hour from 18:00 to 06:00.
- Belmullet (Mayo, further south-west): 14,000 to 20,000 ft until 22:00, then cloud at 2,500 ft.

## What it shows

- The airports' "no cloud" was true and useless: their sensors stop at about 12,000 ft (Belfast) or
  aren't reported above 5,000 ft (Derry), and the cloud was at about 24,000 ft.
- The only sources that measured the high cloud's height were Met Éireann's ceilometers, and the
  telescope was being dimmed by it from about 19:00.
- Precipitation radar, which shows rain and not cloud, showed nothing. The satellite saw cloud, but
  can't say how high or how thick.
- Malin Head went clear at 23:00 while the telescope stayed clouded out until it packed up. A
  ceilometer sees one spot straight up, 50 km away. Finner, further away, kept seeing high cloud all
  night. One point upwind is not a map.

An infrared sky sensor at the telescope would have measured that thin high cloud directly, every
minute, instead of it being worked out from what the other sources didn't see. That is what
cloudyoke's stations are for.

## The collector

Met Éireann's feed published nothing after 14:30 on the 10th until about 14:00 on the 11th, then the
whole backlog at once. The collector only looked 3 hours back and missed it. The readings above were
recovered by hand with `--hours 26` before they aged out, and the collector now reaches back to the
newest reading it already has.
